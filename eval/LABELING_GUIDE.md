# Labeling guide

One question for every headline: for the stock in brackets, is this
good news, bad news, or neither? Judge only the headline. Don't look
the story up.

## bullish (u)
Good for the stock: the price rose, results beat expectations, an analyst
upgraded it or raised the target, a big deal or approval, strong sales.

## bearish (d)
Bad for the stock: the price fell, results missed, a downgrade or lower
target, a lawsuit, probe, recall or outage, weak sales.

## neither (n)
Everything else:
- Not about the business: shopping deals, product reviews, how-to
  articles, local news, sports, entertainment.
- The word means something else: the Amazon river, "wet AMD" (an eye disease).
- The news is about another company: "JPMorgan downgrades Nike" is
  neither for JPMorgan.
- Routine filings and fund trades: "$AAPL Shares Sold by X LLC".
- No direction: earnings previews, "what to expect", questions with no
  answer ("Is it time to buy?").

## Tie-breakers
1. Mixed headline: go with what the stock price did. "Shares fall 10%
   despite strong earnings" is bearish.
2. Mixed, with no price move: neither.
3. Can't decide in about five seconds: neither.

The same headline should get the same label on any day. Being consistent
matters more than being right about the market.
